# Offers 21, "The Stitch": cinematic script

## The idea

The Bridge says people meet a business in pieces. Offers answers it: we join the pieces.

The page is a surface, and the hero's barbed wire comes back whole as a steel thread that stitches through it. The thread is the one 3D object; everything printed on the page stays flat.

At each service the thread makes the stitch that service describes. The section ends by pulling back to show that every frame was one composition, sewn together by one thread. The thread then carries on, off the page, toward what comes next.

## Rules of the world

- **The page:** one invisible surface at z = 0. Anything of the thread behind it is hidden, so the thread visibly goes into and out of the page, like a needle through cloth.
- **The camera:** it always faces the page square, so type never tilts. It moves by tracking, pedestal moves and dolly in and out; the depth lives in the thread.
- **Stops:** the thread moves only between stops. At a stop it is still, and the paragraph arrives word by word; the paragraph leaves before anything moves again.
- **Red:** red appears only where the thread holds something: the lens's subject, the light inside the knot, and the full stop of the last sentence.

## Beats (q = scroll position minus 4.8, measured in screen heights)

| q | Beat | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 2.50 | Bridge's last frame | Untouched. |
| 2.55–2.95 | The sentence becomes a line | Each line of the Bridge sentence flattens into a dash of its own width and slides to the middle, in reading order. The words become dashes, and the dashes close into one cream rule. This is the Bridge's own origin (the About's timecode line) in reverse. |
| 2.95–3.15 | The line becomes steel | The rule runs out to both edges and thickens into the twisted steel of the hero's wire, which is whole again. |
| 3.05–3.50 | It grows barbs | Barbs sprout along it from the centre outward. The camera pushes in slightly, so the twist reads as real depth. |
| 3.55–3.90 | Stop: the heading | "What you can" rises out of the line above it, and "hire us for." drops out of it below. The sentence is split by the steel. |
| 3.90–4.80 | Websites | The camera tracks right along the thread. Ahead of it, the thread starts whipstitching round the giant word "Websites": even arcs over the letters, each one diving through the page. Stop: the word bound by hand. |
| 4.80–5.70 | Photography | The thread leaves the word and winds into a cone that rises off the page toward the viewer. As the camera arrives the cone pulls into focus, its rings tightening onto a red point: a lens. "Photography" sits beneath the near rings. Stop. |
| 5.70–6.60 | Rebuilds | The thread dives through the lens's centre into the page. The camera drops to the next row, where the thread comes back out across a word that has come apart: "Rebu" and "ilds", laced across the gap. On arrival the lacing is pulled tight and the halves close into one word with a seam. Stop. |
| 6.60–7.50 | Care | Night falls as the camera tracks left. The thread ties itself into a knot standing off the page, with a red light inside it that switches on in two steps and lights the steel from within. "Care" in red. Stop. |
| 7.50–8.50 | The whole | The lights come back and the camera pulls all the way out. Everything just seen is one poster in two rows: the split heading, the bound word, the lens, the seam, the knot, and the last sentence, "Tell us what you need". A single thread runs through all of it. It passes through the red full stop and leaves the page at the bottom edge, toward the next section. Stop: the final frame. |

## Why each move happens

- **The line:** the sentence condenses into the thing that will carry the section.
- **Barbs:** it is the hero's wire.
- **Websites:** handmade binding, for "built by hand".
- **Photography:** focusing.
- **Rebuilds:** an honest repair, a fix rather than a replacement.
- **Care:** a knot that holds.
- **The pull-back:** what the stitching was making.
- **The exit:** the line does not end.
