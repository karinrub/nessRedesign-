# D: Independent critique of the Offers wave directions

All judgements are based only on the rendered frames: the five approved audit frames, the rejected storyboard-C, the twelve final shots, and the A motion sheet.

## What the approved sections set up

The approved sections set three rules. The wire is a chunky chrome object that shares the space with the type (p_0, p_1). Red is a full field, not an accent (p_3, p_5). The section just before Offers (p_8) ends on a single thin horizontal barbed wire on black. That last frame is the strongest brief in the folder. Whatever Offers becomes, it should start out of that one line. The rejected storyboard-C failed because of its many rib structures. They read as tunnels, slinkies and ductwork, not as water.

## A, Stroke (one wire curling into a lip)

This is the only direction that a stranger would call "a wave" within half a second. The motion sheet proves the core idea. In m_0.00, the p_8 wire rises, crests and curls (m_0.20 to m_0.39) with the camera fixed. That is a real scroll-driven story, and it continues the approved page instead of starting over.

**Biggest visible problems**
1. **The lip is a knot, not a curl.** In A-v1 the tip doubles back over the inner loop at about x 930–1070, y 430–630. Two wire thicknesses overlap there, the barbs pile up, and the cut end sticks out at about (1050, 625). The same tangle appears in A-v3 at (800–920, 430–590) and in m_0.45/m_0.52. A crashing lip needs one clean taper, not a pile-up.
2. **The "barrel" view (A-v2) is only a crop.** It is the same flat 2D curve scaled up, with a red dot floating in the hollow at (660, 435). There is no depth, no tube and no camera move, so the second view adds nothing.
3. **The back half of the motion falls apart, and the services look alike.** From m_0.58 to m_0.80 the wire collapses into awkward V-kinks: a crimp at about x 1590 in m_0.58, and the notch at m_0.64/m_0.70. Those frames look like broken wire, not a wave receding. The Care frame (m_0.90) is almost the same shape as the Websites frame (m_0.20). That leaves two of the four service moments without their own shape. The final reveal (A-v4) is A-v1 plus a red dot and a headline. It is an ending with no climax.

**Worth borrowing into the others:** the single-line origin. Every direction should open from the exact p_8 wire, in the same position and at the same thickness.

## B, Frames (a row of wave profiles on a rebar spine)

**Biggest visible problems**
1. **The main composition reads as a spring, not a sea.** In B-v1 the overlapping loops at (580–1080, 350–660) look like a stretched coil or a Slinky. The heavy dark rebar cutting diagonally across the bottom right is a second, competing horizon, and it pulls the whole image off balance.
2. **The barrel view is chaos.** B-v2 mixes wire weights badly. Huge foreground strands frame the image while thin strands float at the centre. The red dot is buried behind wires at (715, 450). There are loose cut ends at (1140, 515) and (1370, 540), and the rebar fuses into a messy node at (420–600, 520–640). Nothing tells the eye where to look.
3. **The final reveal collapses into a scribble.** In B-v4 the frames overlap into a crumpled line along y 470–670, and the red dot sits inside the tangle at (818, 618). It looks like the sculpture failed rather than resolved. In B-v3 the last frame is also clipped hard at the right edge (x 1400). Each profile reads as a hook or question mark, not as water.

**Worth borrowing into the others:** the B-v3 sequence, with wave stages laid out left to right like a flipbook. It is the clearest four-part structure in the whole set. It could work as a small progress index or as a ghosted trail in A or C, so each service gets its own wave stage.

## C, Tension (parallel wires stretched between a rebar spine and a curled lip)

C is the most beautiful and most grown-up object here. It reads as engineered and taut, and the C-v3 layout (Websites) is the best type and object balance of all twelve shots. C-v2 is the single best frame in the folder. It looks up through a real tube, with the lip overhead and the red dot sitting like a sun in the mouth of the barrel. C-v4's switch to a full red field matches the red approved sections exactly. It is the only reveal that feels like an event.

**Biggest visible problems**
1. **From the side it does not read as a wave.** In C-v1 and C-v3 the shape is a flattened teardrop with ribs. It reads as a harp, a sail, a wing or a paperclip. The curl that should say "breaking" is a small circle at about (950, 620) in C-v1, at roughly 4% of the frame. This is exactly the failure that sank storyboard-C: ribbed structures read as engineering, not water.
2. **There are render and material defects in the hero frame.** In C-v2 the giant foreground wire across the top (x 450–1440, y 0–300) shows faceting and stretched, low-poly highlights. The ground wires end in needle points at (500, 548) and (580, 548). In C-v1 and C-v3 the dark rebar arc crossing the ribs at about (130–200, 520–740) looks like a structural accident.
3. **The reveal is a background swap, not a transformation.** C-v4 is the C-v1 sculpture repositioned on red. On red, the dark grey rebar at the bottom left (0–300, 520–790) turns muddy and dirty. Because the sculpture is static and only the camera moves, all four service moments risk being four crops of the same object.

**Worth borrowing into the others:** the C-v2 camera and the C-v4 red field. Going inside the tube, then having the background flood to red, is the cinematic payoff that A and B both lack.

## Ranking and recommendation

1. **A, Stroke.** Develop this one.
2. **C, Tension.** Best craft. Its form is wrong for the job.
3. **B, Frames.** No view lands. Keep only its sequencing idea.

I would develop **A**, even though C has the higher raw total. A's weaknesses are execution problems: a knotted lip, a flat barrel view, ugly mid-morph kinks and a thin ending. All of them can be fixed in geometry and camera work. C's weakness is the form itself. Parallel ribs read as a structure, and the client has already rejected that once. No amount of polish turns a harp into a wave.

The A to build: the p_8 wire rises and curls through one clean, tapered lip, with no doubling back. Each service gets its own wave stage, following the B-v3 sequence (swell, peak, curl, throw). The real C-v2 move goes inside A's curl, with the red sun in the tube. The ending is C-v4's flood to red as the lip closes. That merges the strongest borrowings into one direction, and A stays the spine.
