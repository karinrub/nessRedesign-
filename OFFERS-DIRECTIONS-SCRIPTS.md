# NESS Offers: three directions with full scripts (brainstorm, nothing built)

Written 2026-10-18. This replaces the first set (Key, Lighthouse, Sundial). The 26 mockup is rejected and not a basis for any of this.

## 0. What the research changed

**The rule we failed.** A shape is only earned if it is what a verb *leaves behind*. So every direction below is built the same way: the wire does something physical, the camera watches, and the shape is the residue.

**Principles borrowed from film, art and craft (sources at the end)**
1. **A persistent graphic** (the light in *Citizen Kane*): the red dot stays pinned to one screen position across chapter changes, so each hand-off is a graphic match cut, not a fade.
2. **Powers of Ten**: scale is the story. Close-ups of parts, then one pull-back that shows the whole.
3. **Volume from lines** (McCall's cones of light, Sandback's lines, Echelman's "volume without weight"): the finale shape can be *implied by strands* and the eye completes it. This is how many strands read as one sculpture without becoming a mesh.
4. **Density completes it** (Shiota: "when I can no longer trace a yarn with my eye it begins to feel complete"): keep strand counts low enough to trace, never so many they fuse.
5. **Visible mending** (kintsugi, sashiko): Rebuilds shows the repair; the fix stays visible and honest.
6. **Two-colour discipline** (Sleep Well Creative: SOTD Jan 2026, two-colour palette, animations scored 9.0 by the developer jury): ink and cream carry everything, red is the only event.

**Copy split used in all three** (verbatim, only broken into a hook and a reveal)

| Offer | Hook (centered, first) | Reveal (second) |
|---|---|---|
| Websites | "Your website should look like it was made for you, because it was. Every page is planned around the one thing your business needs it to do, then built by hand and tuned to load fast on a phone, since that's where most of your customers will find you." | "When it's finished, the whole thing belongs to you. Domain, files, everything." |
| Photography | "Stock photos make a real business look like a stranger." | "Isabella takes the pictures herself, at your place, of your work and the people behind it, and she shoots with the site already in mind. That is why the two feel like they were born together." |
| Rebuilds | "Maybe you already have a site, and somewhere along the way it stopped doing its job." | "Send it over. You'll get an honest read on whether it needs rebuilding or just fixing, and if a fix is enough, a fix is what you'll get." |
| Care | "Launch day is not goodbye." | "Updates, small fixes, a new page in busy season, a question at nine at night. Call, and one of us picks up, on Maui time. Nobody hands you off to an account manager, because there isn't one." |

**Every chapter has three beats.** HOOK (copy lands, wire is quiet or building) → TURN (one signature set piece, one camera idea) → REVEAL (second copy arrives as the camera lands). No two chapters share a wire verb or a transition.

**Section clock (proposal).** Each chapter gets two stops (hook, reveal); the section grows from about 10 to about 13 screens. Stops, in section time q: heading 3.9, Websites 4.7 / 5.5, Photography 6.3 / 7.1, Rebuilds 7.9 / 8.7, Care 9.5 / 10.3, whole 11.2, end 12.0, hand-off 12.8. The wire moves only between stops; at a stop everything is still and the copy is read.

**Standing rules kept:** palette ink, cream, red; Instrument Sans only, all type flat; barbed wire the only 3D thing; wire never snaps (only in the hero); no wire frames, borders or containers; no package sheet or table at the hand-off.

## 1. Direction I: THE HANGING MODEL ("the form that finds itself")

**Logline.** Gaudí never drew his vaults. He hung cords loaded with small weighted bags, let gravity decide the shape, then turned the picture upside down: the hanging shape becomes a standing one (Hooke's old idea: as the flexible line hangs, so, inverted, the rigid arch stands). We hang NESS's strands from *your* business, let *your needs* decide the form, and at the end flip it: the thing that hung now stands.

**Why this earns the final shape.** The shape is not designed, it is *found*, which is the literal meaning of "made for you, because it was": hang a different weight and you get a different form. The four weights hung over four chapters become four peaks when it flips. The silhouette is a record of the four services.

**Look.** Black void. Strands fall from beyond the top of frame (the anchors are never seen). Silver wire, rim-lit from upper left, sparse barbs. One continuous net the camera tracks along from left to right, so chapters are slides, not cuts.

**The wire.** About 48 to 60 strands, same topology in every state, relaxed offline into five states (plumb, bell, scalloped, sagged, tied), interpolated on scroll. The tension pulse and ghost versions use the existing per-ring draw-time shader attribute (brightness wave along each strand).

### Entrance (Bridge to heading, q 2.6 to 3.9)
The Bridge's lines press into bars, converge on one cream horizontal line. The line pivots about its centre to vertical and *becomes the first plumb line*; 47 more drop in beside it, one every few frames, forming a curtain of perfectly vertical strands ("plumb": true, planned). Heading "What you can hire us for." sits left. The dot is a pinprick at the foot of the first strand.

### Chapter 1 WEBSITES: verb HANG
- **Hook (stop 4.7).** Paragraph one centered in the dark. Behind the words the plumb lines hang quiet.
- **Turn.** "made for you" slides out of the paragraph into the red circle. The red circle *is the weight*: it lowers into the curtain and every strand is pulled toward it, short and tight at the edges, long and slack in the middle: a *bell* hangs around the dot, the text sitting inside its hollow. Three dim ghosts of other bells (narrow tall, wide shallow, lopsided) hang beside, flicker, and fade: no two hang alike. Camera pushes down into the weight; strands stream past; red fills the frame.
- **Reveal (stop 5.5).** Paragraph two on the red. Then the red shrinks to a dot at the bottom of the bell and the camera tracks right to the next place on the net.
- **Hand-off.** A track (not a cut): the net continues to the right.

### Chapter 2 PHOTOGRAPHY: verb WEIGH-IN
- **Hook (stop 6.3).** "…like a stranger." The net ahead holds twelve *identical* small weights spaced evenly: a perfect repeating festoon, forgettable, every business the same.
- **Turn.** The twelve weights slide along their strands to irregular positions, and swell or shrink: some large, some tiny, clustered where real people and real places are. The net *flows* into an irregular, specific, graceful surface. The first bell (from Websites) dimples because the new weights pull on it: neither the photos nor the site can be moved without moving the other. Rack focus: foreground weights sharp, the rest dimmed.
- **Reveal (stop 7.1).** Paragraph two, set where the net is emptiest. "Born together" lands as the last weight settles.
- **Hand-off.** A slow dolly to the right where the net sags.

### Chapter 3 REBUILDS: verb TIE
- **Hook (stop 7.9).** "…stopped doing its job." One span has sagged under accumulated load: strands slack, wavy, dim.
- **Turn (the honest read).** A bright pulse runs along the top, then down each strand in sequence: taut strands ring bright, slack ones stay dull, so the tension map draws itself. Verdict, shown in two takes: a ghost of the *entire* net lifting off and re-hanging (the rebuild), which fades as not needed; and then one single new strand crosses the sag, anchors both sides, and *tightens*. The sag rises like a held breath.
- **Reveal (stop 8.7).** Paragraph two. The tie stays: straight among curves, slightly brighter: a visible mend, never hidden.
- **Hand-off.** The camera rises slowly with the sag, up and right.

### Chapter 4 CARE: verb SWAY
- **Hook (stop 9.5).** "Launch day is not goodbye." Night: key light drops to a quarter, the dot warms. The net breathes in a slow wind; strands shimmer faintly.
- **Turn.** "A question at nine at night": one far strand is tugged. The ripple runs *straight* along the nearest path to the weight, and the weight nudges by a hair. The net re-settles with a small adjustment (small fixes). The dot brightens: picked up. Three grey, still weights far to one side never move (no handoffs).
- **Reveal (stop 10.3).** Paragraph two.
- **Hand-off.** The weights release one by one (their pull goes), the net quivers.

### Finale: THE FLIP (stops 11.2 and 12.0)
The camera rolls 180° about the horizontal axis while the text stays upright and flat: the world turns over around the words. The hanging bells are now *rising spires*, a ridge of four peaks of different height and width, because the four loads were different. The first one, the heaviest, is the tallest. The camera pulls back and rises: one complete shape, four spires joined by one net, with the dot as a sun low in the saddle between the two middle peaks. CTA "Tell us what you need" sits in the sky left.
**Hand-off to Packages.** The camera climbs over the ridge; the sun rises and fills the frame. Packages begins in dawn-red. Nothing hangs a sheet.

**Beauty cues.** The catenary is the most elegant curve there is; a bell of strands with a clear hollow for type; negative space; the flip is a single unforgettable move.
**Risks.** Photography is the most abstract chapter (the slide from regular to irregular must be gorgeous to carry it). The finale may read as "cathedral" or "mountains": both fine, never literal. Needs a short test of the flip so it feels like turning a model over, not a camera trick.

## 2. Direction II: THE ORB ("a web built for one place")

**Logline.** A web is built around one point, anchored to the branches that happen to be there, and nowhere else would it fit. At sunrise every drop on it holds a tiny upside-down world. The red circle is the hub and the sun at once.

**Why this earns the final shape.** The orb has exact answers for all four offers:
- **Websites**: spun around a hub, anchored to *this* place's irregular frame, built strand by strand (bridge line carried by the wind, then a Y, then the hub, radii, spiral).
- **Photography**: dew forms on the web only; each drop is a lens that shows an inverted image of what is behind it. Photographs that exist only because the structure holds them.
- **Rebuilds**: the web reads tension through its threads; a sag is corrected by re-tensioning, not rebuilding.
- **Care**: a spider at the hub reads vibration from any thread through the tips of its legs. A tug anywhere is felt at the centre instantly, directly, with nothing in between.

**Look.** Pre-dawn macro. The red sun low behind the hub, rim-lighting every strand. The outer frame lines run off-screen at irregular angles, so the web is visibly custom to its surroundings.

**The wire.** About 60 strands (radii and spiral segments), drawn on in the real construction order. Dew is not extra geometry: it is *glints on the barbs* (bright specular points that move with parallax). The dot is the only disc.

### Entrance (q 2.6 to 3.9)
The cream line (the bars' end) hangs between two unseen anchors and *relaxes into a curve*: it is the first bridge thread. A second strand drops from its middle, pulls tight: a Y. The dot is a pinprick at the junction (the future hub). Heading appears.

### Chapter 1 WEBSITES: verb SPIN
- **Hook (stop 4.7).** Paragraph one holds the centre. The web is spun *around the words*: radii start at the edge of the text block (as if the text were the hub) and run to the irregular frame; they draw on one by one.
- **Turn.** A bright point travels the spiral from centre outward, laying the capture line behind it (the web is "built by hand"); the pace ramps from slow to very fast ("tuned to load fast on a phone"). The paragraph dissolves to "made for you", which condenses into the red hub. The sun rises behind it, so the red circle is hub and sun. Camera pushes into the hub, spokes stream past, red fills.
- **Reveal (stop 5.5).** Paragraph two on red. The red irises down to a hub dot.
- **Hand-off.** The camera drifts along one radius, outward, in the pre-dawn.

### Chapter 2 PHOTOGRAPHY: verb CATCH LIGHT
- **Hook (stop 6.3).** "…like a stranger." A clip-art web appears in the centre: perfectly regular, symmetric, anchored to nothing. Cold, dim. It dissolves.
- **Turn.** The real, irregular web returns. First light: glints bead along the spiral one by one. Each glint carries a tiny inverted red dot (the sun), and as the camera drifts the dots slide across the drops in sync (parallax): each drop is a photograph of the same morning from a slightly different place. Rack focus: nearest strands sharp, rest soft.
- **Reveal (stop 7.1).** Paragraph two. "Born together": the drops can form only on the web.
- **Hand-off.** The sun clears and every drop flares at once; the web looks made of light; the flare fades to the quiet of Rebuilds.

### Chapter 3 REBUILDS: verb TRUE
- **Hook (stop 7.9).** "…stopped doing its job." Overnight rain and wind: radii wavy and slack, spiral loops uneven, the dew gone, the strands dull.
- **Turn.** A pulse travels around the hub clockwise, testing each radius: taut ones ring, slack ones stay dull. A ghost of an entirely new web lays itself over and is declined. Then each slack radius is re-tensioned in turn with a small tick, curved to straight, the way a wheel is trued: the whole web tightens into crisp geometry. Two radii stay a hair brighter: new silk, a visible mend.
- **Reveal (stop 8.7).** Paragraph two.
- **Hand-off.** The tension holds; the light fades; evening.

### Chapter 4 CARE: verb LISTEN
- **Hook (stop 9.5).** "Launch day is not goodbye." Night. The hub is a small warm dot.
- **Turn.** "A question at nine at night": one far strand trembles. A kink travels along *one radius* to the hub; the hub answers with a soft pulse outward through all the radii, and the dot brightens: picked up. Two other webs far off keep dark hubs; the ripple goes past them.
- **Reveal (stop 10.3).** Paragraph two.
- **Hand-off.** Dawn grey begins.

### Finale (stops 11.2 and 12.0)
The camera pulls back through layers of webs at different depths, each a different shape and each with its own red hub, in dawn light: one meadow of custom webs; ours is the sharp one in front. CTA in the sky. **Hand-off:** the camera flies into our hub, which opens to the sun; red fills; Packages begins.

**Beauty cues.** A web backlit at sunrise is one of the most beautiful natural images there is; low strand counts you can trace; real parallax shimmer.
**Risks.** "Web for a website" is the oldest pun in the industry: only the construction, the dew and the hub-as-listening-post save it. Spider-fear (we never show a spider). A planar web can look flat: the layering of several webs at depth in the finale and the macro parallax in Photography are doing that work. The fake dew inversion must read as light, not as extra objects.

## 3. Direction III: THE KEEPER'S LIGHT (a night of lens and beam, no tower)

**Logline.** A lighthouse has one job, and someone keeps it every night. We never show a tower, only the lens and the beam. The whole section is one night, dusk to dawn, and the red dot is the flame.

**Why this earns the final shape.** Keepers lit the lamp at dusk, trimmed the wicks, wound the clockwork weights that turned the lens at least once each evening, polished the glass, and closed the lantern curtains by day so the lens would not burn. The shape comes from tending: rings, beam, sweep.

**Look.** Deep night blue-black (stays ink), a lens built of concentric rings around the flame, a cone of strands. Camera inside the lantern for the whole night until the finale.

**The wire.** Concentric Fresnel-style rings (about 14 strands, each a closed loop that is not a perfect circle), a cone of about 70 radial strands for the beam, a handful of long strands for the clockwork's drive. Beam brightness is the shader's story light.

### Chapter 1 WEBSITES: verb RING
- **Hook (stop 4.7).** Paragraph one centered. Quiet.
- **Turn.** Rings draw outward around the flame, one by one, each loop slightly hand-irregular and a little different from the last: planned around one thing. A few rings re-draw to "fit the one thing" (they cinch toward the flame until snug; barbs click). "made for you" sits in the flame. Camera pushes into the flame; red fills.
- **Reveal (stop 5.5).** Paragraph two on red; the red contracts back to a flame.

### Chapter 2 PHOTOGRAPHY: verb FOCUS
- **Hook (stop 6.3).** "…like a stranger." A wide, even fan of beam strands goes out in the dark and lands on nothing: a generic light.
- **Turn.** The rings re-space to match the flame (ground for it); the fan *tightens* as the strands converge to a pinpoint on one exact place (a literal rack focus made of strands). "Born together": lens and flame are made as a pair.
- **Reveal (stop 7.1).** Paragraph two.

### Chapter 3 REBUILDS: verb RE-AIM
- **Hook (stop 7.9).** "…stopped doing its job." The flame gutters; the beam drifts off target and splays.
- **Turn.** One slow survey sweep around the whole horizon (the honest read), then a ghost of a whole new light rising out of the dark is declined; a small nudge of the lamp re-aims the beam: the fix. The sweep's rotation carries the camera into Care.
- **Reveal (stop 8.7).** Paragraph two.

### Chapter 4 CARE: verb KEEP WATCH
- **Hook (stop 9.5).** "Launch day is not goodbye." The beam turns like a clock; a drive strand pulls through the dark (the clockwork wound).
- **Turn.** "A question at nine at night": a small ping from the dark water; the beam turns to it and holds, then resumes.
- **Reveal (stop 10.3).** Paragraph two.

### Finale (stops 11.2 and 12.0)
The camera slides out of the lantern: the full shape is the lens and its cone of strands sweeping across the dark, one complete sculpture of light (McCall's cone, implied by strands). The beam finds a distant small red light on the water; the light *comes toward us*. **Hand-off:** it fills the frame: a customer arrives; Packages begins in red.

**Beauty cues.** Night, big sweeping geometry, strong silhouettes, the most dramatic of the three.
**Risks.** Lighthouses are a familiar brand image (mitigated by never showing the tower). Fresnel rings and the web both use concentric rings: do not combine. Fully nocturnal; the wire must still read in the dark.

## 4. Comparison

| | I Hanging Model | II Orb | III Keeper's Light |
|---|---|---|---|
| Final shape | Ridge of four spires, flipped from a hanging net | Meadow of custom webs at dawn | Lens and beam cone sweeping the dark |
| Story depth | Found form, tie rod fix | Exact answer to all four offers | Exact answer to all four, with keeper's duties |
| Originality | Highest | Medium (web pun) | Medium (lighthouse familiarity) |
| Mood | Calm, architectural | Intimate, luminous | Nocturnal, dramatic |
| Weakest chapter | Photography | Photography (dew) | Websites (rings vs the print idea) |
| Build cost | Medium (relaxation states plus the flip) | Medium (construction order plus glints) | Low to medium |

**Motif available to any direction:** the twist. Barbed wire is two strands twisted so the barbs stay put. A twist wave running down two strands is the cleanest "born together" in the material itself, and can replace the Photography turn in any of the three.

**Retired from the first set, and why:** the Key (literal, only a few chapters mapped well), the Sundial (the idea was too subtle to read in a second).
